Fundamentals

Angle, speed & distance

Every process, every joint, comes down to the same three things held steady: the angle you hold the torch or electrode, how fast you travel, and how far the tip sits from the work. Waver on any one and it shows up in the bead.

Travel angle — push vs. drag

Tilt forward (push, left) for a flatter bead with less penetration; tilt backward (drag, right) for a narrower, deeper bead. Typically 5–15° off vertical. If there's slag, you drag — stick and self-shielded flux-core always trail the puddle so slag doesn't get trapped underneath. MIG and TIG can go either way.

Work angle

The side-to-side tilt, usually bisecting the joint — roughly 45° on a fillet, straight down the middle on a butt joint — so heat is split evenly between both pieces instead of favoring one.

Distance & travel speed

Keep stick-out or arc length consistent (roughly the electrode's diameter for stick, 2–3× tungsten diameter for TIG, 3/8–1/2" contact-tip distance for MIG). Move steadily — too slow piles up heat and undercut-prone puddles, too fast starves the joint of fusion.

Quick technique reference

One habit that matters most

Watch the puddle, not the arc

The molten pool tells you more than any settings chart. Watch its back edge, width and how it wets into the joint — if it's ropey and narrow, you're too cold or moving too fast; if it's flat, wide and washing out the edges, you're too hot or too slow. Use the calculator to get close, then let the puddle do the fine-tuning.
